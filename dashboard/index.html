Live comparables: what launched tokens actually trade at

The model learns from these — the snapshot's launched protocols, by market cap. Their market-cap-to-TVL multiples are the comparables behind every implied value on the board. A protocol whose token trades is never ranked as a launch: it is the market's own answer, shown here for comparison.

Method

The value model

Implied value is a comparables model fit on the snapshot's launched protocols (the 128 in it with a market cap), by ordinary least squares in logs:

log(market cap) = a + b × log(effective TVL) + category effect

Effective TVL is the reported TVL with half of a 7-day jump discounted: value is put on the TVL that stayed, not on the spike that arrived yesterday. Falls are never rewarded. The fitted slope is about 0.52, so value grows with the square root of TVL, not with TVL itself: a flat multiple overvalues the giants and undervalues the small. The category effect is the median residual of the launched protocols in the category, shrunk toward zero with four pseudo-observations, so a category with one or two launched names carries no effect at all.

What the data says, and what it does not

  • Size dominates: log market cap on log TVL has a slope near 0.5 and a correlation near 0.4. Category moves the multiple next: launched Lending names sit near a 0.13 multiple, Dexs near 0.34, RWA near 0.28.
  • Seven-day growth does not lift the multiple in the launched set — if anything it trims it, because a TVL spike is often mercenary capital. That is why growth enters only as the spike discount above, never as a reward.
  • Age does not predict the multiple at all (correlation about 0.00), so age does not move the value. It sets the farm plan's hold window instead, and it is shown on every row.
  • Chain count does not predict the multiple either. Chains are shown so a reader can farm where they already hold funds.

The backtest

Before the board is trusted, the fit is checked on the same snapshot by leave-one-out: every launched protocol is held out in turn, the fit is redone without it, and the prediction is scored against its real market cap. The dashboard's model card shows the result beside the same test run on a flat global-median multiple. A model that cannot predict the launches that already happened is not asked to price the ones that have not.

The board

The board covers the whole watchlist (every protocol at or above the $50,000,000 floor with no token and not a wrapped, staked or bridged product of a token that trades) and every other rankable pre-token protocol in the snapshot below the floor — the early radar. Wrapped, staked and bridged products are left out because their underlying token already trades. Each row carries six farm steps derived from the protocol's own record (category, chains, TVL, age), with what each step costs and how long it takes.

Limits, stated plainly

  • Implied value is modelled on circulating market cap, the only value the snapshot records for launched tokens. A fully diluted value would usually be higher.
  • The snapshot is frozen at 2026-11-14. Nothing here is live, and nothing here predicts whether or when a protocol issues a token: the board prices a launch if one happens, at the record as it stands.
  • A farm plan is a starting point from public categories, not financial advice. Never pay to claim an airdrop, and claim only from a link the protocol itself publishes.
